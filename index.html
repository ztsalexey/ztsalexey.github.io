Origin story

Gravity is overrated

I grew up in a small town deep in Siberia, where minus forty is just weather and a good math or physics problem was the best entertainment in town. I did a lot of those. They got me to a high school in New York, where I captained the soccer team, then to Purdue to learn how to leave the planet, and eventually onto a plane that flies parabolas so I could test hardware I'd built in zero gravity. Gravity turned out to be overrated. So did winter, but don't tell my hometown: these days I'm in the Scottsdale desert, where the only snow is on my GitHub banner.

Aerospace

  • Flew as crew on a Zero-G parabolic flight to test the small-satellite propellant pumping system I built.
  • Designed the geometry of a blood-air separator prepared for flight on Blue Origin's New Shepard.
  • Led life support for a 70-person Mars cycler in a propellantless-exploration study led by Dr. Andy Aldrin and Dr. James Longuski.
  • Laser diagnostics of plasma in Dr. Richard Miles' lab at Texas A&M.

Education

  • Purdue University, B.S. Aeronautical & Astronautical Engineering
  • Purdue University, M.S. Engineering Management
  • Texas A&M University, Aerospace Engineering PhD program. Left to build full-time.

First company

  • Back home in Siberia, started a lemonade business in 2017: built our own carbonation rig from industrial parts, cut the start-up capital ~10×, and broke even in the first week.

Still that kid from a small Siberian town who can't leave a hard problem alone. Building OpenOrange now; fastest way to reach me is X or LinkedIn.
